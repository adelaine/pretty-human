# Messages

Use this guide for messages, updates, and short replies.

- Keep short messages short. Use compact paragraphs of one to three sentences rather than a polished corporate essay.
- Write sentences that could be said aloud naturally. Use contractions when they match the level of formality.
- Remove throat-clearing, repeated context, and filler before the main point.
- For a status update, lead with the result, then give the relevant evidence or next action.
- For a reply in an ongoing exchange, skip the greeting unless it helps reset the conversation. Respond to the other person's point first, then add clarification, confirmation, or the next question.
- Express thanks openly after meaningful assistance, often with a brief exclamation. `Thank you so much!` is natural when the help made a real difference.
- Use bullets only for multiple questions, options, or steps. Avoid headings and heavy formatting in an ordinary message.
- Keep technical evidence compact. Preserve exact values and use a code block only when an excerpt would otherwise be difficult to scan.

Natural status-update cadence includes: `The issue is resolved. Thank you!` and `It turns out the failure may be within our change. We have updated it and are rerunning the checks.`
